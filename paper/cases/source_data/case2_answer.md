## Conclusion first

Across roughly two millennia of materia medica, Huangqi (黄芪/黃耆, *Astragalus* root) accumulates a stable core — **sweet (甘) and mildly warm (微温), non‑toxic, ascending‑and‑descending (可升可降) "yang within yin" (陰中陽也)** — around which later physicians build four distinct functional roles: **tonify the middle and raise the clear yang** (补中升阳), **secure the exterior and stop spontaneous sweating** (固表止汗), **generate blood and flesh / tonify pus and support the interior of sores** (生血生肌、排脓内托), and **dispel wind‑damp and unblock the channels** (祛风胜湿, for 血痹/风湿). Its trajectory is one of *expansion* rather than change: it begins in the Han as a single agent for chronic suppurating ulcers and "deficiency," and by the Ming–Qing becomes the leading qi‑tonic for the Li Dongyuan school of spleen‑stomach deficiency. The classical cautions are likewise stable and date back to the earliest full statement of them (Miao Xiyong, 1625).

| Qing, Xu Dachuan, *Shennong Bencao Jing Baizhong Lu* | 「味甘微溫。主癰疽，久敗瘡，排膿止痛。（除肌肉中之熱毒。）…補虛。（補脾胃之虛。）」[[jc_c044.00075]] | "Tonics of deficiency" defined specifically as **tonifying the Spleen‑Stomach** |
| Republican, Peng Ziyi, *Yundong de Gud Zhongyixue* | 「黃耆大補衛氣之陽，乃瘡科補虛之藥。…肺虛忌用。…肺氣主降，黃耆性升故也。」[[jc_o127.02430]] | The sharpest late critique: contraindicated in lung deficiency because Huangqi *rises* while lung qi *descends* |

**On tropism there is no consensus.** The corpus gives at least four incompatible systems: Yangming–Taiyin (Miao Xiyong), gallbladder/sanjiao/lung/spleen (Chen Xiuyuan), Foot‑Taiyang + Hand‑Taiyin (Zhou Yan), and Wang Haogu's functional three‑burner scheme — 「黄耆治氣虚盗汗并自汗及膚痛是皮表之藥…治路血柔脾胃是中州之藥…補腎臓元氣是裏藥乃上中下内外三焦之藥也」[[bencao_gangmu_skqs.036.01516]] (quoted in *Bencao Gangmu*). All agree, however, that the drug acts **from the centre outward** and is governed by the Spleen‑Stomach–Lung axis.

## 2. Therapeutic actions and indications as they accumulated

**Han → Tang: the wound‑suppurant and "deficiency" agent.** The Han text already pairs Huangqi with chronic suppurating ulcers, wind‑scabies, haemorrhoids and scrofula, "deficiency" and "the hundred disorders of children" [[jc_c000b.00065]]. Lu Zhiyi's *Bencao Chengya Banji* restates the same clause almost verbatim [[bencao_chengya.001.00112]] — evidence of how canonically stable this entry was.

**Ming: the whole materia medica is re‑organised around qi.** Miao Xiyong gives the mechanism: 甘 (sweet, earth's own flavour) → 解毒 and 益血; 陽 (yang) → 達表、運毒走表; 脾主肌肉 → 久败疮; 甘温益元氣, 甘温除大熱 → 通治五劳羸瘦 and 止渴; 血虚 → 腹痛; 中焦不治 → 泄痢 [[bencao_jingshu.007.01404]]. Wang Haogu adds the three‑burner pharmacology above; Li Dongyuan adds the decisive physiological link: 「黄耆既補三焦實胃氣與桂同功特比桂甘平不辛熱為異耳…又黄耆與人參甘草三味為除躁熱肌熱之聖藥脾胃一虚肺氣先絶必用黄耆温分肉益皮毛實腠理不令汗出以益元氣而補三焦」[[bencao_gangmu_skqs.036.01516]].

**Qing: the "raw vs honey‑fried" doctrine becomes the standard of use.** Wang Ang's *Bencao Beiyao* is the fullest Qing statement:

- 「生用固表，無汗能發，有汗能止（丹溪云：黃耆大補陽虛自汗，若表虛有邪，不汗出者服此又能自汗。）溫分肉，實腠理，瀉陰火，解肌熱。」[[jc_c018a.00028]]
- 「炙用補中，益元氣，溫三焦，壯脾胃（脾胃一虛，土不能生金，則肺氣先絕。脾胃和緩，則肺氣旺而肌表固實，補中即所以固表也。），生血生肌。（氣能生血，血充則肉長。經曰：血生肉。）排膿內託，癰瘡聖藥。」[[jc_c018a.00028]]

Zhu Shu's *Bencao Jing Shuzheng* keeps Huangqi inside the "sores and ulcers" cluster alongside 络石, 白敛, 乌喙, 通草, 败酱 [[jc_c046.01985]].

**A key therapeutic rule — who is sovereign, who is minister.** Chen Jiamo: 「人參補中黄耆實表凡内傷脾胃發熱惡寒吐泄怠卧脹滿痞塞神短脉微者當以人參為君黄耆為臣若表虚自汗亡陽潰瘍痘疹隂瘡者當以黄耆為君人參為臣不可執一也」[[bencao_gangmu_skqs.036.01518]] — i.e. the two drugs swap priority by pattern; this is the hinge between Li Dongyuan's middle‑tonifying school and the exterior‑securing school.

### Processing and grades

- **Raw vs honey‑fried**: 「入補中藥，搥扁蜜炙，達表生用。」[[jc_c018a.00029]]; in the corpus 2,693 formulas, the recorded preparation notes are 锉 (346), 炒, 炙, 蜜炙, 去芦, 细锉 — honey‑frying dominates from the Ming/Qing onward.
- **Grades and adulterants**: 绵耆 (best), 白水耆 (cold‑tonifying), 赤水耆, 木耆 (inferior: 「莖短理橫，功力殊劣；（此為下品。）」[[jc_c012.00118]]); Li Shizhen warns against苜蓿根 as a substitute, and Wang Haogu adds 「苜蓿根味苦。堅脆宜審」[[jc_c034.00226]].

## 4. Contraindications and cautions

**The earliest complete statement is Ming (1625), Miao Xiyong's 简误 — and it is remarkable for being framed as "by what mechanism it harms":**

「黄耆功能實表有表邪者勿用能助氣氣實者勿用能内塞補不足胸膈氣閉悶腸胃有積滯者勿用能補陽陽盛隂虚者忌之上焦熱甚下焦虚寒者忌之病人多怒肝氣不和者勿服痘瘡血分熱盛者禁用」[[bencao_jingshu.007.01406]]

Decoded: (1) do not use with **exterior pathogen** — it "secures the exterior" and would trap it; (2) do not use in **excess qi** — it "assists qi"; (3) do not use where there is **chest/diaphragm obstruction or food stagnation in the intestines** — it "fills and obstructs"; (4) contraindicated in **excess yang with yin deficiency**; (5) contraindicated when **upper burner heat is severe with lower burner deficiency‑cold**; (6) avoid in patients with much anger and disharmonious liver qi; (7) **absolutely prohibited in smallpox with heat exuberance in the blood aspect**.

**Later additions:**

- Wang Ang: 「表旺者不宜用，陰虛者宜少用，恐升氣於表而裏愈虛矣。」[[jc_c018a.00029]] — excess exterior, or yin deficiency, use sparingly, for fear of raising qi to the surface and leaving the interior emptier.
- Zhu Danxi: 「黄耆補元氣肥白而多汗者為宜若面黒形實而瘦者服之令人胸滿宜以三抝湯瀉之」[[bencao_gangmu_skqs.036.01516]] — suited to the fat, pale, easily-sweating person; contraindicated in the dark‑complexioned, constitutionally full and thin person, in whom it causes chest fullness.
- Peng Ziyi (Republican): 「肺虛忌用…肺氣主降，黃耆性升故也。」[[jc_o127.02430]] — contraindicated in lung deficiency, because Huangqi ascends while lung qi descends; he also warns that pairing Huangqi with Angelica as a generic "qi and blood" tonic is a widespread abuse.
- **Compatibility relations** (汪昂): 「茯苓為使，惡龜甲、白蘚皮，畏防風。」[[jc_c018a.00029]]

**On the Eighteen Incompatibilities / Nineteen Antipathies:** the corpus's incompatibilities check returns **no pair in which Huangqi appears** — i.e. Huangqi is not named as a member of any 反 or 畏 statement in the Eighteen Incompatibilities or Nineteen Antipathies material. The classical verse for antipathy names 「人参最怕五灵脂」, which concerns *Ren Shen*, not Huangqi. This is a negative finding from the literature, not a safety clearance.

**Modern safety note (material flagged in the texts):** Huangqi itself is not among the toxic materials flagged, but two canonical Huangqi formulas contain aconite‑class drugs — 乌头汤 (川乌) [[jc_b100.00134]] and the 《千金》三黄汤, whose 「先有寒加附子一枚」 [[jc_b100.00138]]. The safety reference records for 附子: **cardiotoxicity and neurotoxicity (aconitine alkaloids); proper processing and long decoction are required, and misuse can cause arrhythmia** [[附子 entry]]. This is a warning about those drugs, not about Huangqi, and it is not a recommendation.

## 5. What remains uncertain and needs human verification

1. **"Earliest witness" ≠ historical first.** The earliest *玉屏风散* in this corpus is Dai Sigong (1443) [[jc_f022.01352]], but the formula is commonly attributed elsewhere (《究原方》/丹溪 tradition) — *Yuan Yuan Fang* is not in this corpus. Likewise *Renzhai Zhizhi* (1264) attributes 当归六黄汤 to 《聖惠方》, and the widely circulated composition is Xue Ji's 1529 version [[xueshi_yian.010.01899]].
2. **Two witnesses of *Bencao Gangmu* exist here** (the 上册 witness and the 卷十二上 witness); collation status is flagged unverified, and section headings differ (「釋名」/「释名」). Quotes above are taken from the 卷十二上 witness.
3. **Tropism has no consensus** — Yangming‑Taiyin, gallbladder‑sanjiao‑lung‑spleen, Foot‑Taiyang/Hand‑Taiyin and the three‑burner scheme coexist [[bencao_jingshu.007.01404]][[jc_c000c.00003]][[jc_c045.00061]][[bencao_gangmu_skqs.036.01516]]. The corpus's own herb‑property survey records tropism statements in only 4 of 13 works consulted, so most of the tradition simply does not state it.
4. **Doses are historical readings.** All gram figures quoted (e.g. "黄耆四兩" ≈149 g Qing reckoning) are conversions for reading old texts, not doses.
5. **Textual layer caution:** the *Bencao Gangmu* "发明" section is a compilation of earlier physicians' statements (Haogu, Dongyuan, Danxi, Jiamo, Zengjieshi); I have attributed each explicitly rather than treating them as Li Shizhen's own doctrine.
6. **No modern-therapy equivalence is implied.** Terms such as 自汗, 盗汗, 中风, 消渴 here are historical categories; I have not mapped them to any modern diagnosis.
