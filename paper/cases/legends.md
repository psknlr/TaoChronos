# Case figure legends

Three answers of the conversation agent 道乃久 (Tao-R1) on the full corpus (version f9df290db65b, 23 November 2026),
one figure each. Legends follow the Nature layout and are under 300 words. In every figure, characters between 「 and 」
are quoted verbatim from the passage named; other characters are the terms the English renders. Passage identifiers,
every plotted value and the answers themselves are in `source_data/`.

## Case Fig. 1 | From the features of a case to a pattern, a formula and its modifications.

**a**, The question as put to the agent, and the witnesses its answer quotes, by composition date (bar, a date range
longer than 60 years; area, passages quoted). **b**, Pattern differentiation as the answer argues it: each feature of
the case, its classical reading, the passage quoted for it and the part of the pattern it supports — the disease and
its site (grey), the branch (orange), the root (blue). **c**, *Duhuo jisheng tang* drug by drug against three
neighbours. Rows, the five functional clusters the answer takes from Wang Ang's commentary (1682), in two arms: five
drugs expel the pathogen, ten support the root. Filled circles, the composition the comparison tool read; open
circles, a drug in some witnesses only. Cards, the indication quoted, the drugs shared with *Duhuo jisheng tang* and
the Jaccard index of the two compositions, and the answer's verdict. †Xuduan is counted in *Duhuo jisheng tang*: one
witness names it as the substitute for Sangjisheng, another as an ingredient. ‡Huangqi is written in two of four
(*Sanbi tang*) and one of four (*Juanbi tang*) witnesses that give doses. **d**, Witnesses of the formula in the
corpus, and the 41 compositions written out under its name. **e**, Modifications on record: adjustments of the formula
(blue), agents for deeper disease (grey), cautions (vermillion). **f**, Findings named before the formula in the
treatment statements of nine works, with their lift: a computed association, not a proof. **g**, Outcomes in the 11
case records that name the formula. **h**, What the answer leaves to examination. A reconstruction from historical
texts, not a prescription.

## Case Fig. 2 | Huangqi through the materia medica: a stable core, a growing repertoire.

**a**, As in Case Fig. 1a. **b**, What the answer reports, by period (columns) and by property or therapeutic role
(rows); a band runs from the earliest witness cited for a row. Formulas (blue) stand at their earliest witness in the
corpus, which need not be their first appearance. Flavour and nature are those of 13 materia medica surveyed; four
state a tropism. Captions under the periods paraphrase the answer's own headings. **c**, Compositions that contain
Huangqi among those written out (2,693 of 69,744, in 101 works), by period: share and 95% Wilson interval; dashed
line, all periods. Cochran–Armitage test for a trend across the ordered periods, z = 4.16, *P* = 3.1 × 10⁻⁵. **d**,
Doses written by weight for Huangqi in decoctions (n = 991 compositions), converted at the mid-point of each period's
estimate of the liang: points, compositions; box, interquartile range; line, median; right, the form written most
often. Kruskal–Wallis H = 77.1, *P* < 0.001; Spearman correlation with composition date, ρ = −0.34,
*P* = 8.0 × 10⁻²⁸. **e**, Odds ratios (Woolf 95% intervals) of twelve partners in the same composition: the ten the
answer discusses, Gancao and Fangfeng; all significant by Fisher's exact test after Benjamini–Hochberg correction.
**f**, Written doses in *Buyang huanwu tang* (1848). **g**, The seven cautions of Miao Xiyong (1625), four of them
derived from an action of the drug (grey, the text's wording), and later statements. Counts are of the corpus as
transcribed; where a transcription leaves a blank for the second character of 黃耆 the drug is not counted, so they
are lower bounds. Converted doses are scholarly estimates for comparing texts, not dosage guidance.

## Case Fig. 3 | Low back pain: what the agent released, and what it withheld.

**a**, As in Case Fig. 1a. **b**, The acupuncture case review returned "information insufficient": the question
states neither excess nor deficiency, and an unknown is not read as a negative. The answer therefore gives no
prescription and assigns neither supplementing nor draining; it sets out the tradition's framework with its witnesses.
**c**, The points of that framework on a schematic figure seen from behind, placed by surface landmarks, for
orientation only: local points (A, blue), distal points on the bladder channel (B, orange), the confluent pair (C,
green) and the extra point named only by the tool's rule table (grey). Shaded, the site of pain. **d**, For each
point: its class, the classical statement quoted, its place in the rule table (main or adjunct) and the number of
works in the corpus that give it for lumbar pain. The 14th and 16th vertebrae of the classical count are L2 and L4.
**e**, The pairing methods among the six points of the rule table. **f**, Earliest witness in the corpus of each
selection rule (the lower bound of its composition date); the rule on local with distal points is a textbook rule
whose passage is in the Inner Classic. **g**, The five shu points of the bladder channel with their phases, and the
rule each state of the patient would call on; mother, child and own-phase points are those of the tool's table.
**h**, The safety statements of the answer. Traditional reasoning from texts: not a diagnosis, not a prescription and
not evidence of benefit.
