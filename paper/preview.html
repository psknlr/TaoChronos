Fig. 1 | TaoChronos (道乃久): agents propose, validators grade, experts decide.

a, The harness as configured (web-canon profile), by principal (rows) and by the stage of a research round (columns). A scientist's question becomes a research contract, hashed before the first task. The Director plans each round and picks, from fifteen specialists, the agents the kernel spawns; each reads only through the research's scoped view and calls only the tools its permissions allow (letters). Filled circles, agents that may consult a language model where their rules leave a case uncertain (Fig. 3 called none). The Skeptic's objections return to the Hypothesis agent, which may only narrow a claim. Every write is an event in the log. Hooks (vermillion) refuse a tool call or result outside the research's scope, a claim whose quote is not verbatim or contradicts it, an equation of a historical with a modern concept, and a report on versions the contract never judged. Validators alone grade claims, gates G0–G6 and G8, scores and the contract's verdict, and close a hard objection only by re-running its check; the expert alone sets G7. b, Event authority: the principals allowed to emit each group of the 61 event types (number of types); the reducer refuses any other event. c, One clause of the Jingui yaolüe (chapter 13) in three witnesses, aligned character by character: the Deng Zhen print (1340), the Wu Qian manuscript (1395), which lacks 小便一斗, and the Yuan print of the Maijing (1330), which writes 圎 for 丸, a Song taboo form that dates its exemplar after 1126; shaded, variant positions. Below, the provenance chain of the first witness from claim to licence; the conversation client checks every cited passage (✓ verbatim, ≠ quotation differs, × no such passage). CMETA, 數位中醫校書郎. Agents, tools and permissions: Supplementary Tables 4–8.

Fig. 2 | A dated, layered corpus spanning two millennia, and the canon its citations define.

a, Construction of the corpus. Transcriptions from ten sources pass admission (historical texts only), de-duplication against more trusted sources (normalized 12-character shingles; independently collated transcriptions are kept as separate witnesses), layer-aware dating (main text, commentaries, editors' notes and later additions dated apart; undated material counted as Qing, never earlier) and normalization used for matching only. b, Witnesses per 25-year bin by composition date; each witness counts once, its weight spread evenly over its date range (n = 966; blue, the 531 witnesses of the 350 canon works). Bands mark the analysis periods; the last, unlabelled band is the Republican period (1912–1949). c, Basis on which each witness was dated; the 104 undated witnesses are counted as Qing (vermillion), never earlier. d, Influence score of the 350 canon works against the midpoint of their composition range; point area, witnesses in the corpus (capped at 10). Score = 10 log₂(1 + citing works not older than it) + 2 × periods in which it is cited + 2 log₂(1 + citations) + 3 log₂(1 + witnesses) + 6 × inclusion in the Siku quanshu; the ten highest (blue) are keyed by rank. Later works have had fewer chances to be cited: the score describes what the corpus cites, not a verdict on the tradition. e, Characters kept for each witness of the open web corpus against the canon rank of its work (log–log). Dashed line, the budget of a work's first witness (350,000 characters for ranks 1–10, then 50,000, 25,000 and 12,000); blue, texts kept whole; triangles, second and third witnesses of the ten highest-ranked works. Data: corpus catalogues (corpus/catalog) and the web corpus pack (corpus/web).

Fig. 3 | One research question, end to end.

a, The question, put to the harness on the open canon corpus (web-canon profile; rules only, no model called), and the contract fixed before the first task. b, The 30 tasks in the order the Director planned them over three rounds: agent tasks (blue), validator tasks (grey); numbers, tool calls (the Skeptic's are quotation checks). Below each round, the evidence records, new books and hypotheses it added; the run stopped when new evidence fell below 5% in the last round the contract allowed. c, The 14 hypotheses proposed in round 1 (bands, one per hypothesis): the Skeptic kept 3, sent 10 back to be narrowed and rejected 1; validators then judged the 13 candidates against the contract. Below, the Skeptic's 117 objections by check: closed by a validator once the claim was narrowed, left open (minor or interpretive) or critical. d, The final hypotheses against the gates and the contract's criteria. Twelve fail on independent sources (G5): passage reuse, closed transitively, puts 35 of the 38 books that supplied evidence into one source family (Supplementary Fig. 1). e, Three outcomes with their verbatim evidence. H1 meets every criterion a machine can check and waits for an expert. H2 rests on five witnesses of one clause, which the source-dependence check counts once. R1 is rejected because a later book restates the link, although neither passage gives 理中 for fever itself: the rules read co-occurrence in a passage as association. A second run writes the same report; the report, the event log and every hypothesis and objection are Supplementary Data 1–2 and Supplementary Tables 9–10. D1–D5, discovery tracks: lost knowledge, concept evolution, formula evolution, hidden associations, contradictions.

Fig. 4 | Quotations stay verbatim, the harness's boundaries hold, and hard readings stay safe.

a, Quotation checks on 60 random passages of the demonstration corpus: verbatim quotations accepted; quotations with one character replaced, of random characters, or verbatim at a shifted span rejected. Dots, proportions; lines, Wilson 95% confidence intervals. b, Nineteen invariants (I01–I19) run as probes on the intact harness and on six variants, each with one boundary removed by deleting its check at run time. Every removal broke the invariant named for its boundary (outlined) and no other; 127 of 133 cells held. In three cases a second safeguard limited the damage: without the scope guard the tool it no longer refused returned nothing of the withheld passage; forged G7 events reached the log but not the state; without the bound checks two of five impossible evidence records verified. c, 107 hard interpretive cases in eight classes, each a verbatim quotation with one correct reading (development, 23 cases used to write the rules; held-out, 84). Numbers, held-out cases read correctly. Both errors were unsafe, a reading that commentators dispute accepted as supported (the Dahuang mudan tang clause and the 里水 of the Yuebi jia zhu tang clause); they are reported, not patched. d, Accuracy in four blind rounds, each on new cases written before the rules that first read them, and on all held-out cases under the current rules. Lines, Wilson 95% confidence intervals; below, correct/total and unsafe errors. Data: taochronos eval on the demonstration corpus (a, c), paper/figures/mutation_matrix.py (b) and docs/evals.md (d).

Fig. 5 | Rankings computed from the past anticipate the links later physicians wrote.

a, Blind temporal benchmark on the full corpus. At each cutoff (1127, 1368) the store splits into passages dated wholly before and wholly after it; passages that straddle it belong to neither side. For every disease or symptom of the lexicon with 150–6,000 past and at least 300 future passages, the formulas and drugs of its past graph not yet linked to it are ranked; a candidate comes true when claims in two or more later books link it to the topic. b, Precision at 10 and 20 and mean average precision of five rankers over the 189 topic–cutoff pairs in which at least one link came true. Dots, means; lines, 95% confidence intervals; chance, 20 random rankings; dashed line, base rate (4.5%). c, Paired comparisons of average precision over topic–cutoff pairs; two-sided sign tests, one tie excluded. d, Predictions of the whole pipeline run on the past view at 1368 for four held-out topics (胸痹, 霍乱, 虚劳, 痰饮): proportion borne out by later books, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Open circle, before the absence checks, which rejected two wrong 'lost' and two already attested 'hidden' associations and no discovery. No passage outside the past view was read (leakage 0). Values of the full-corpus run documented in docs/evals.md; the full store is not distributed, and Extended Data Fig. 2 repeats the benchmark on the open corpus.

Fig. 6 | On the open canon corpus, the study layer recovers history written into the texts.

All panels are computed by the deterministic study layer on the open web corpus (348 works, 135,083 passages). a, b, Share of works per century writing an original drug name and the name a naming taboo imposed: 薯蓣 and 山药 (a), 玄参 and 元参 (b). Dashed lines, the years from which the names were avoided: Tang Daizong (李豫, 762) and Song Yingzong (赵曙, 1063) in a; the Song ancestor Shengzu (赵玄朗, 1012) and the Kangxi emperor (玄烨, 1662) in b; shading, the switch interval the tool detected; marker area, works in the century; open markers, centuries with fewer than three works or only undated front matter dated conservatively to 1911. A work writing both names counts for both. c, Passages mentioning 温病 (warm disease) per 10,000 passages by period, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals; numbers, passages with the term. Two-sided Cochran–Armitage test for trend across the ordered periods. d, Normalized pointwise mutual information (NPMI) of drug pairs co-occurring significantly in 303 compositions (a composition repeated in copies of one work counted once; pairs in at least ten works tested; one-sided Fisher exact test, Benjamini–Hochberg q < 0.05; 63 pairs). The groups the tool finds without formula names (outlined) coincide with the cores of classical formulas (right); liquorice (甘草), present in most formulas, joins none. e, Citations between physicians: arcs join citing and cited physicians placed at the midpoints of their dates; width ∝ √citations; blue, citations of Zhang Ji (张机). Five links whose cited physician lived after the citing one (28 citations) are omitted as chronologically impossible.

Extended Data Fig. 1 | Where the rules transfer and where they do not.

a, Development or constructed data (open circles) against held-out or real-corpus data (blue) for nine capabilities; numbers, the two scores; †, real-corpus value from the full corpus (docs/evals.md). Development sets were written with the rules they test. b, Sentence-boundary F1 of the learned punctuation model and of the rule baseline on the demonstration corpus (four folds), on 55 works held out of training, and on four unpunctuated Siku texts scored against their JiCheng punctuation (†, full corpus); every character was preserved in all settings. c, Ablations on the demonstration corpus: earliest-attestation accuracy with retrieval routes removed (6 terms), contradiction typing without sense resolution or philology (12 passage pairs), the context supplied to agents, and review without the Skeptic.

Extended Data Fig. 2 | The research-value benchmark on the open canon corpus.

The benchmark of Fig. 5 re-run with identical rules on the open web corpus (348 works, most sampled by chapters). a, Average precision of Adamic–Adar (blue), popularity (grey) and the expectation under chance (open circles) in each of the 30 topic–cutoff pairs in which at least one link came true (18 at 1127, 12 at 1368; log scale); lines join Adamic–Adar to popularity, blue where Adamic–Adar is higher; right, links that came true in the pair. Most pairs have one or two true links (56 among 6,178 candidates), so a single hit moves a ranking. b, Precision@10 and mean average precision of the five rankers, both cutoffs pooled (n = 30 pairs); dots, means; lines, 95% confidence intervals; dashed line, base rate (0.91%). c, Paired comparisons of average precision, two-sided sign tests. Adamic–Adar still ranks above popularity (24 of 30 pairs, P = 0.0014) but cannot be told from chance (13 better, 17 worse, P = 0.58), and popularity falls below chance (6 better, 24 worse, P = 0.0014). The benchmark needs the full corpus; the replication is reported as a limit of the open subset. Every pair and every prediction: Supplementary Tables 11 and 12.
